LinkedIn carousel, 6 slides, 1080 × 1350 px (4:5 portrait).
Source of truth for every diagnosis fact: the Founder Clarity Report for NyayaSetu AI, generated 26 Sep 2026 (`clarity-report-15.pdf`, 11 pages).

Nothing in this document that describes the diagnosis is invented. Every score, band, root cause, quote and action below is copied from the report. Where the report is silent, the slide stays silent.

## 1. Content summary of the report

**What it is.** A Founder Clarity Report, Sep 2026, for Ayush Roy on NyayaSetu AI. Ally scanned 15 business dimensions and 6 readiness pillars, read 4 free-text answers given before the diagnosis plus 30 diagnosis answers, and traced what is holding the business back to one root cause.

**Certainty caveat (page 1).** The report opens with "A note on certainty": the session did not gather enough signal to be confident, so the whole read is a provisional draft, not a settled diagnosis. Bands reflect how much evidence supported the read, not how well the founder is doing.

**Business Health (page 1).** Overall band: **Needs Attention**. Framed as "Where you are today — not a verdict on whether this works." Revenue Maturity is carrying the most weight.

**Founder DNA (pages 2–3).** Archetype **Problem Solver**, core motivation **Purpose**. Origin: legal information is hard for ordinary people to understand or access. Vision metric: weekly active users who come back and use NyayaSetu again. Decision style: "figuring it out as I go"; one rushed decision was moving to the prototype before fully validating the target user. Last shipped: the NyayaSetu prototype on the website. Hard-won lesson: building a good product does not automatically mean people will use it. Communication preference: straight first.

**Business DNA — six pillars (pages 3–5).** Flagged for immediate attention: Founder Readiness, Revenue Maturity, Strategic Clarity.

| Pillar | Weight | Band |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue Maturity | 20% | Not started yet (Demand Reality, Revenue Model Clarity and Pricing Confidence only) |
| Founder Readiness | 25% | Needs Attention |
| Strategic Clarity | 10% | Needs Attention |
| Team & Leadership | 10% | Needs Attention (Team Structure & Role Clarity and Decision Rights only) |
| Market Clarity | 20% | Needs Attention |
| Product & Execution | 15% | Developing |

**Dimensions ranked (page 5).** Lean on this: Sales & Revenue (Strong), Strategy & Planning (Strong), Team & Leadership (Strong). Fix this first: Go-To-Market (Developing), Founder Psychology (Developing). "This is where the next two weeks should go."

**Root cause (pages 6–8).** Ally's finding, **moderate confidence, unconfirmed**: **"Building Is the Safe Place"** (Founder Psychology, primary). Supporting, also unconfirmed: **Lack of Data** (Fundraising) and **No System for Tracking Real Behavior Against Model Assumptions** (Business Model Design). The founder's own problem statement: "the biggest problem right now is users… I need real users to validate whether the product genuinely solves their problem, and right now I don't have enough of them." Reasoning chain: Product & Execution scored higher, so this is not a capability gap; Founder Psychology carried the heaviest risk of every dimension; the same pattern showed up across unrelated answers; one mechanism explains the rest.

**Basis (page 8).** 4 pre-diagnosis answers + 30 diagnosis answers. The three findings are "leading hypotheses, not verdicts."

**Priority actions (pages 9–10).** Three confirm-first actions: (1) write the problem you solve in one sentence, who has it and what changes for them, before naming what you built; (2) list your three strongest claims and the specific external thing that backs each, marking the ones with nothing; (3) explain the business out loud to someone who does not know it and note the exact moment they looked lost. Sequence: "Two weeks, one question to answer." Week 1: actions 2 and 3. Week 2: name the single most likely alternative someone would use instead (including nothing), write one plain sentence on why they'd choose you, then delete your business name and check it could not be anyone else's.

**What the report deliberately leaves out (page 11).** Expected impact: "Ally leaves a section out when it does not have enough to say something true there, rather than filling it in."

**The real report's visual language (used as the design reference).** Cream page (~#F5F2EC) with a dark forest green hero card (#0F2A1E → #14352A), a small green pill label ("FOUNDER CLARITY REPORT · SEP 2026"), high-contrast serif headline, a semicircular gauge labelled BUSINESS HEALTH, and bands shown as thin progress bars with a text label at the right. Status colours in the report: muted terracotta for Needs Attention / Not started yet, amber for Developing, emerald for Strong, lime-green bar for Moderate confidence. The reference image mentioned in the brief did not arrive with the upload; the report's own hero card is used in its place.

## 3. Shared visual system (read before any slide prompt)

### Canvas
1080 × 1350 px, portrait. Safe margins 88 px on all sides. Nothing important within 120 px of the bottom edge (LinkedIn overlays the page counter there).

### Colour tokens
| Token | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `bg` | #0B1F17 | Slide background, near-black forest green |
| `bg-card` | #12291F | Dashboard card surfaces, one step lighter than bg |
| `green-deep` | #1C3D2E | Card borders, dividers at 40% opacity, grid lines |
| `emerald` | #2ECC71 | Primary accent: pill labels, active markers, progress fills, the ✓ marks |
| `mint` | #BFE8D2 | Secondary accent: eyebrow labels, italic emphasis in headlines, small metadata |
| `text` | #F3EFE6 | Warm off-white headline and body text |
| `muted` | #8FA69A | Desaturated green-grey for labels and secondary copy |
| `status-attention` | #C8664A | Muted terracotta. Only inside dashboard status bars for Needs Attention / Not started yet. Taken from the report. |
| `status-developing` | #C9973C | Muted amber. Only inside dashboard status bars for Developing. Taken from the report. |

The two status colours exist because the report uses them and the band data cannot be read without them. They appear only on slide 2 and, at small scale, slide 4. They never touch typography or backgrounds.

### Typography
- **Headline serif:** a high-contrast editorial serif with a slight warmth (Fraunces, Playfair Display, or Tiempos Headline). Weight 500–600. Tight leading (0.98). Slight negative tracking (−1%).
- **UI sans:** a clean neo-grotesque (Inter, Söhne, or Geist). Labels in 500 weight, all caps, +8% tracking. Body in 400.
- Scale, applied identically on every slide: eyebrow 22 px caps · headline 96–120 px serif · sub-headline 34–38 px sans · dashboard labels 20–24 px · dashboard values 26–30 px · footer 20 px caps.

### Recurring elements
- **Eyebrow pill** top-left on every slide: 1 px emerald outline, rounded, emerald caps text, 22 px. Text changes per slide (listed below).
- **Footer** bottom-left on every slide: `GOXL | ALLY` in muted caps, 20 px. Bottom-right: slide counter `01 / 06` in muted, same size.
- **Diagnostic grid** on slides 1 and 6 only: a faint 12-column grid of 1 px lines in `green-deep` at 18% opacity, plus a single thin semicircular arc echoing the Business Health gauge, 6% opacity, partially off-canvas top-right. On slides 2–5 the grid is absent so the dashboard elements carry the structure.
- **Card style** for any UI element: `bg-card` fill, 1 px `green-deep` border, 20 px radius, no drop shadow, no glow.
- **Progress bars**: 6 px tall, fully rounded, track in `green-deep`, fill in the status colour, text label to the right in the same colour, 22 px sans 500.

### Absolute exclusions (put verbatim in every prompt's negative section)
No robots, no humanoid figures, no brains, no circuit boards, no neon, no cyberpunk, no lens flares, no glassmorphism blur, no 3D renders, no stock photography, no gradients beyond the subtle vertical bg tone, no icons except ✓ and →, no confetti, no rockets, no lightbulbs, no charts that are not specified.
